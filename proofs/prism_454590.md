# The 45-45-90 water prism with beam parallel to the hypotenuse

Configuration (Kit, 2026-10-28): a 45-45-90 triangular prism, white
source with the beam **parallel to the hypotenuse**, refractive index
set to water. Worked as pure geometry in `prism_454590.py`
(18/18 checks, exit 0): exact symbolic derivation for general n plus
an independent numeric segment ray tracer with known-true and
known-false controls. Local only.

## Setup

Apex A = (0, 1) (the 90° corner), B = (−1, 0), C = (1, 0); hypotenuse
BC is the base. Beam direction (1, 0), entering leg AB at the point
(−u, 1−u), u ∈ [0, 1] measured from the apex. Because the legs stand
at 45° to the hypotenuse, the entry incidence is forced to exactly

- i₁ = π/4 (the tie angle), sin r₁ = √2/(2n), c := cos r₁, μ := 1/n.

Internal ray: d₁ = (μ/2 + sc, μ/2 − sc), s = √2/2. Note d₁x + d₁y = μ.

## Results (PROVED as classical ray geometry)

**Channel boundary.** The ray reaches the hypotenuse first iff
u > u*, the far leg first iff u < u*, with

- u* = μ/(2sc) — for water (n = 4/3) u* ≈ 0.62554.

The boundary ray passes exactly through corner C. (Symbolic P5;
tracer bisection agrees, T7.)

**Channel A (u > u*) — the mirror channel.** The ray totally
reflects off the hypotenuse: incidence π/4 + r₁ there, and
sin(π/4 + r₁) > μ ⟺ n > 1, so TIR holds for every real material.
It then meets the far leg at internal incidence **exactly r₁**, so
Snell at exit gives sin i₂ = n sin r₁ = √2/2, i₂ = π/4, and the
emergent ray is

- **exactly (1, 0) — parallel to the incident beam, for every n.**

Zero angular dispersion in this channel, proved for general n
(P1–P7), not sampled: the prism is a total-internal-reflection
mirror (the Porro configuration), deviating the beam by pure lateral
displacement. Colors separate inside (r₁(λ)) and in the displacement
and in u*(λ), never in the exit angle.

**Channel B (u < u*) — the hypotenuse-exit channel.** The ray meets
the far leg at incidence π/2 − r₁; TIR there ⟺ c > μ ⟺ n > √(3/2)
≈ 1.2247 (water qualifies). Reflecting, it reaches the hypotenuse at
incidence **π/4 − r₁** and refracts out with the exact law

- sin i₂ = n sin(π/4 − r₁) = n(sc − μ/2), exit direction angle i₂ − π/2.

This channel is n-dependent and **disperses**: for water the exit sits
at −72.60° (d line) with an F−C spread of 0.3005°.

**Correction disclosed.** The first hand derivation of channel B
claimed a second TIR at the hypotenuse and an emergent ray of (0, 1).
That was wrong: it mistook sc + μ/2 for a sine; it is cos(π/4 − r₁).
The independent tracer refuted the claim (T2 failed on the first run)
before anything was reported; the symbolic identities about the
hypothetical reflected ray were true algebra under a false premise.
Channel B above is the corrected treatment (P10–P11, T2/T3/T6).

**Water numbers** (i₁ = 45° throughout): r₁ = 32.028° (n = 4/3),
32.037° (d, 1.3330); critical angle 48.59°; channel-A hypotenuse
incidence 77.03°; u* from 0.62310 (F) to 0.62700 (C). The internal
fan r₁(F) − r₁(C) = 0.1611° exists inside the prism even where the
exit cancels it.

**Newton's regime, same prism.** Direct leg-to-leg passage (the
classic dispersive path, apex 90°) is blocked at 45° entry by the
channel-B TIR; it opens only at steep entry (for water, entry
> 61.81°), with minimum deviation 50.98° at entry 70.49° (d line)
and an F−C spread at minimum deviation of 1.466° — roughly five
times channel B's fan and infinitely more than channel A's zero.

## Scope

Ray geometry only. Total-internal-reflection phase shifts (the
Fresnel-rhomb effect), Goos–Hänchen shifts, and all wave optics are
outside this note. No identification with the model's operators is
claimed: the entry angle being the tie π/4, and channel A's
cancellation being an equal-internal-angles balance, are structural
rhymes with the record's tie machinery — noted as rhymes, at most
witness-grade. The refractive index remains an empirical Γ-grade
input (the 2026-10-16 screen stands: Γ = 4/3 as the exact water index
is FALSIFIED; 1.3330 is the measured d-line value).
